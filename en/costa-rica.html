San José — theatre and museums

Culture and history
Worth a stop

The National Theatre, Pre-Columbian Gold Museum and National Museum introduce Costa Rica’s history and culture. Combine the theatre with one museum for a day in the capital.

Time needed4–6 hours
Physical effortEasy
Details

Start with the National Theatre, then choose the Pre-Columbian Gold Museum or the National Museum. A theatre tour explores the interiors, while the museums introduce the country’s culture and history. Walk between visits through the city centre.

Tickets and reservations

The theatre and museums have separate tickets and schedules. A guide is not compulsory for an ordinary National Museum visit; standard tickets can be bought on arrival. Use a dedicated city car park.

City walking, not hiking. Choose one or two museums

Drive to a secure car park or take a taxi; explore the centre on foot. Marker: National Theatre

Driving access: La Antigua car park. Walk to the theatre and museums.

Orosi Valley and Tapantí

Culture and history · Forests and gardens
Worth a stop

Orosi Valley combines a historic church, coffee farms and mountain views. Nearby Tapantí offers walks among rivers and moss-covered trees in wet mountain forest.

Time neededOrosi: 1–2 hours; park: 2–4 hours
Physical effortModerate
Details

Visit the old church in Orosi, see coffee farms and stop at mountain viewpoints. In nearby Tapantí, paths run through wet forest beside rivers and moss-covered trees. Choose an easy path or a steeper route, checking open sections with park staff.

Tickets and reservations

Tapantí requires a SINAC ticket, separate from visiting the valley and villages. Open ordinary trails can be walked without a guide. Check sector hours and parking. Swimming in park rivers is prohibited.

Oropéndola: about 1.2 km. Árboles Caídos: about 3 km and roughly 200 m elevation change, considerably steeper. Ask rangers about distant trails

Drive to Orosi; sections of the approach to Tapantí are gravel.

Driving access: Orosi church. Arrival at the church; a dedicated car park is not confirmed. Follow local signs.

Scenery and viewpoints · Animals and birds

Physical effortModerate
Details

Follow paths and stairs to several waterfalls, then visit the butterfly garden and animal exhibits. For a gentler visit, focus on the gardens and fewer waterfall descents. Animals are kept in care here, so the experience differs from watching wildlife in its natural habitat.

Driving access: La Paz Waterfall Gardens car park. At the main entrance; continue on foot.

Volcanoes, geysers and caves · Scenery and viewpoints
Regional highlights

Arenal’s volcanic cone rises above rainforest and old lava fields. Nearby La Fortuna is a convenient base for park walks, waterfalls and hot springs.

Physical effortModerate
Details

National park trails cross forest and old lava fields, with viewpoints towards the volcanic cone. Nearby private nature parks have their own walks. Climbing Arenal is prohibited; explore the permitted trails around its base.

Tickets and reservations

The national park, Arenal 1968 and Observatory have separate tickets. Check the exact entrance, payment and parking arrangements. There is no all-area La Fortuna ticket.

Coladas: 1.7 km; El Ceibo: 2.3 km. These are separate network sections, not one combined loop. Climbing the cone is prohibited

Ordinary car on main roads; check individual lodge approaches. Directions use Sector Coladas

Driving access: Arenal Coladas sector car park. Lava trails begin beside the car park.

Monteverde — cloud forest

Forests and gardens · Animals and birds
Regional highlights

Moss, plants growing on tree branches, mist and cool air distinguish this forest from the hot Arenal lowlands. Monteverde names both the region and a reserve; services cluster around Santa Elena.

Time neededForest: 3–4 hours; area: 2–3 nights
Physical effortModerate
Details

Mosses and other plants cover the tree branches in this cool cloud forest. Choose a short circuit or a longer trail to viewpoints on the Continental Divide. Distant views depend on cloud cover, but the ferns, damp trunks and birds make the forest worth exploring in mist too.

Tickets and reservations

Paid admission; the official reserve strongly recommends online booking. Leave the car at the Visitor Center and follow the current access arrangements. Confirm shuttle, parking and arrival time.

Official loops: Essence 1.4 km / about 1 hour; Heart of the Forest 2.8 km / 1.5 hours; Continental Divide 4.1 km / 2.5 hours, moderate.

Drive to the Visitor Center about 1.5 km below the forest entrance, then take the included shuttle

Driving access: Monteverde Visitor Center / parking. Take the shuttle to the forest trails.

Rafting on the Pacuare River takes you between forested gorge walls, side waterfalls and rapids. This is an active guided outing for visitors who enjoy time on the water.

Time neededAbout 3 hours rafting; a full day with transfers
Physical effortChallenging
Details

On the Pacuare, rapids alternate with calmer stretches where you can take in forested slopes and waterfalls. Day and multi-day trips are available. Participants paddle to the guide’s instructions; difficulty depends on water levels and the chosen section.

Tickets and reservations

Book a professional operator with equipment included. Check age and ability restrictions, transfers and parking. Unsafe water levels can cause cancellation or a change of river.

Class III–IV rafting, about 30 km with the listed operator. Effort and difficulty depend on water levels.

Organised transfer or drive to the base. Some trips connect different towns; agree luggage arrangements

Driving access: Exploradores Outdoors car park. For Exploradores Outdoors rafting customers, followed by an operator transfer to the river.

Physical effortChallenging
Details

Cloudbridge protects and restores mountain forest, with trails leading through the trees to waterfalls. Choose a short waterfall walk or a longer circuit with steep climbs. These are day walks with a return to the village.

Confirm day-entry and donation arrangements with the reserve and check open trails on arrival. Guided programmes are separate. Follow current administration instructions for parking and access.

Chirripó — a separate mountain expedition

Scenery and viewpoints · Outdoor activities
Complex logistics

Costa Rica’s highest mountain, open páramo and glacially formed valleys. A distinctive landscape but much more demanding than ordinary day walks.

Time needed3–4 days including a preparation night
Physical effortChallenging
Details

Above the forest lies páramo: open high-altitude grassland and shrubland, with valleys shaped by ancient glaciers. Most visitors climb to the refuge first and make a separate summit ascent. Plan your approach, reserve overnight stays and allow flexibility for poor weather.

Tickets and reservations

Advance SINAC reservation required. Accommodation and meals are arranged separately. Local accompaniment is compulsory on Herradura–Urán–Chirripó and San Jerónimo–Base Crestones routes. Agree parking at your trailhead.

A long climb to the refuge followed by a separate summit outing. Altitude, luggage and several days of walking add to the effort; choose your route before booking.

Drive to San Gerardo de Rivas, then hike. The marker shows the summit, not the trailhead

Driving access: Hotel Urán car park. Near the Chirripó trailhead; agree parking and reserve park access ahead.

Why visit

Visit Costa Rica to watch tropical wildlife: listen to the forest at dawn and look for sloths in the canopy, toucans, hummingbirds and brightly coloured frogs. Many animals are well concealed among the leaves, so a naturalist guide can help you spot more.

One trip can take you from humid lowland jungle to Monteverde’s cool, often misty mountain forest, past Arenal Volcano and hot springs to the blue waters of Río Celeste. Explore Tortuguero’s canals by boat, head to the Osa Peninsula for forests and wildlife, or visit the coasts for beaches and surfing. The Caribbean coast also offers Afro-Caribbean food and culture.

Plan early starts for wildlife outings and several nights in each area, leaving time for both forest walks and relaxation by the water. Lowlands are hot and humid, while the mountains are cooler. Heavy rain and road conditions can add considerably to driving times.

When to visit

Pacific coast: December–April is generally drier; May–November brings more rain and lush vegetation.

Caribbean coast: relatively dry weather is more common in February–March and September–October. The seasons here differ from those on the Pacific.

Arenal and mountain forests: rain is possible all year, with cooler temperatures at altitude. Turtle and whale-watching seasons vary by coast; see the individual place cards for details.

Travel essentials

The currency is the Costa Rican colón (CRC). US dollars are often accepted, but local cash is useful for small purchases. Card payments are common in tourist areas. Spanish is the main language; English is often spoken at hotels and on tours, while a translation app can help elsewhere. Socket types are A/B.

Drive on the right. Buses and tourist shuttles connect the main towns. Some remote beaches and parks require a high-clearance or four-wheel-drive vehicle; check road conditions ahead. Plan rural drives for daylight and allow for rain-related delays. Do not leave valuables in the car; avoid deserted, unfamiliar areas after dark.

Check entry requirements for your nationality and prepare proof of onward travel. Arrange medical insurance and check how to reserve entry to popular parks.

Review routine vaccinations and discuss hepatitis A vaccination and other route-specific recommendations with a clinician. Bring repellent if needed, along with a rain jacket, quick-drying clothing, closed-toe footwear and sun protection.

Strong rip currents occur at beaches. Crocodiles live in rivers, mangroves and estuaries, including the Tárcoles and Tempisque rivers and the Tortuguero area: stay out of the water in their habitat. Venomous snakes occur in forests and lowlands; watch your footing and leave animals alone. Bull sharks inhabit waters around the Murciélago Islands off the northern Pacific coast; dives there are led by experienced guides.
