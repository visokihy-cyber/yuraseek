Why visit

Come close to tropical nature: hear the forest at dawn, spot a sloth in the canopy and watch toucans, hummingbirds and brightly coloured frogs. Much of the experience lies in small details: moving branches, bird calls and life beside the trail. A naturalist guide helps reveal what is easy to miss, while every encounter with wildlife is different.

A single trip can combine humid jungle with Monteverde’s cool cloud forest, Arenal Volcano with hot springs, waterfalls and the blue Río Celeste. Explore Tortuguero’s canals by boat, visit the Osa Peninsula for a deeper immersion in nature, or head to the coasts for beaches and surfing. The Caribbean adds its own food, rhythm and Afro-Caribbean culture.

Expect nature-rich days, early starts, humid lowlands and cooler mountains. Rain, cloud and trail conditions influence plans, and drives often take longer than the map suggests. Several nights in one area make it easier to alternate forest walks, water activities and rest. The lasting appeal is feeling surrounded by a living tropical world.

When to visit

Pacific coast: December–April is usually drier; May–November is greener and wetter.

Caribbean coast: relatively dry spells often fall in February–March and September–October.

Arenal and mountain forests: rain is possible year-round, with cooler temperatures at altitude. Turtle and whale watching have specific seasons, noted in the place cards.

Travel essentials

The currency is the Costa Rican colón (CRC). US dollars are often accepted, but local cash is useful for small purchases. Cards are common in tourist areas. Spanish is the main language; English is often spoken at hotels and on tours, while a translation app helps elsewhere.

Drive on the right. Buses and tourist shuttles connect main towns, but access to remote beaches and parks may require high clearance or four-wheel drive. Rain slows journeys; aim to drive rural roads in daylight. Take valuables out of the car and choose busy areas after dark. Watch for strong rip currents at beaches.

Check entry requirements for your citizenship and prepare proof of onward travel. Arrange medical insurance and check advance bookings for popular parks.

Review routine vaccinations and discuss hepatitis A protection and other route-specific advice with a clinician. Bring repellent if needed, a rain jacket, quick-drying clothing, closed footwear and sun protection. Power sockets are types A/B.

Crocodiles occur in rivers, mangroves and estuaries on both coasts, notably around Tárcoles, Tempisque and Tortuguero; do not enter water in their habitat. Venomous snakes live in damp forests and lowlands: watch your footing and leave animals alone. Bull sharks occur around the Murciélago Islands off the northern Pacific coast; diving there requires experienced guidance.
