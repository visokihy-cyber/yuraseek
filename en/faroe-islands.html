Why visit

The Faroes’ defining sight is the meeting of land and ocean: grass ends at a sheer wall, waterfalls plunge into the sea and islands recede in layers. Múlafossur, the lake at Trælanípa and Kallur lighthouse reveal different versions of this landscape. Slættaratindur opens up a broad mountain panorama, while a Vestmanna boat trip shows the coast from below.

Seabirds are another reason to visit in summer. On Mykines, guided walks reach puffin colonies; on Nólsoy, night tours introduce storm petrels. Tórshavn, Kirkjubøur and small island museums add history, painting and a sense of life shaped by the sea.

Sandoy suits a gentler day of beaches and art. Give Suðuroy its own trip for the high coast and Hvannhagi valley. Leaving some flexibility between days helps you see the cliffs and mountain panoramas in clear weather instead of rushing through every island.

When to visit

May–August: the main season for boat trips and seabirds. Mid-May to late August is the best period to plan around puffins. June and July bring very long days; reserve accommodation and popular boat trips ahead.

September–October: fewer visitors, but shorter days and fewer scheduled sailings. Birds leave their breeding colonies, so come for the landscapes rather than puffin watching.

November–April: short daylight hours, strong winds and possible snow or ice on high trails. Villages and accessible roadside viewpoints can still be rewarding, but winter needs a flexible plan. Fog and rain are possible in every month.

Travel essentials

Faroese and Danish kroner circulate at equal value, with prices normally shown in DKK. Cards are widely accepted, though some trail fees require cash. Faroese and Danish are the main languages; English is generally spoken in tourism settings. Socket types are C and K, at 230 V.

Traffic drives on the right. Use passing places on narrow roads and older single-lane tunnels, and watch for sheep. Subsea tunnels charge tolls; ask your rental company how they collect payment. Ferries and sightseeing boats depend on sea conditions, so leave a buffer before your flight.

Check the entry rules before buying non-refundable tickets: an ordinary Schengen visa alone does not permit entry to the Faroes, and residence-permit rules differ. The islands are outside the EU; check mobile roaming charges separately too.

The main outdoor hazards are gusting wind, fog, wet slopes, cliffs and surf. Bring waterproof outer layers, warm clothing, grippy footwear and an offline map. Keep to paths around nesting birds and follow guides’ instructions, as birds may defend their nests. On remote islands, plan the return sailing before setting out.
