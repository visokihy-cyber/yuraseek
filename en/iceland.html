Why visit

Ice, water and volcanoes. Strokkur, Mývatn’s geothermal fields and the glacier lagoon reveal different forces reshaping the island. Many places can be enjoyed from shore; walking on a glacier or entering an ice cave is a separate organised activity.

Views and trails. Southern waterfalls alternate with black beaches, while Snæfellsnes combines lava shores and mountain silhouettes. Choose Landmannalaugar and Kerlingarfjöll for dedicated highland walks, not as quick roadside detours.

Wildlife and culture. Puffins nest on sea cliffs in summer; whale-watching boats sail from Húsavík. Glaumbær’s turf houses, Reykholt and Þingvellir’s old parliament explain how people lived in this landscape. Wildlife encounters depend on season and weather.

When to visit

June–August: long daylight, birdlife and the widest choice of walks. Highland roads open according to conditions, not automatically at the start of summer.

May and September: fewer visitors, but some mountain roads and services are unavailable; allow weather flexibility.

October–April: short days, snow and storms can change plans. Northern lights need darkness and clear skies; ice caves require permitted conditions and an operator. Check the forecast and roads daily.

Travel essentials

The currency is the Icelandic króna (ISK). Cards are widely accepted; unattended fuel pumps may require a PIN. Icelandic is the national language and English is widely used in tourism. Traffic drives on the right. F-roads are seasonal and require suitable vehicles; four-wheel drive does not make river crossings safe. Services and mobile reception can be sparse away from settlements. Socket types: C/F.

Main hazards include wind, cold, surf, slippery cliffs and geothermal ground. At Reynisfjara, stay well away from the water; on bird cliffs, keep back from edges and nests. Observe marine animals from a distance. Pack waterproof layers, warm clothing and suitable footwear; a midge net can help around Mývatn in summer, with repellent if needed. Discuss health preparation and routine vaccinations according to your circumstances before departure. Leave a plan for remote walks; the emergency number is 112. Check alerts and practical information.
