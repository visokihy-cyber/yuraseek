Why visit

The defining impression is a steep island rising from the ocean. Spend the morning on a ridge above the clouds, follow a levada to a waterfall in the afternoon and watch waves beside lava pools in the evening. Levadas are water channels with paths alongside, offering a way into the forests and hillsides.

Choose Pico do Areeiro and Pico Ruivo for mountain panoramas, or São Lourenço for an almost desert-like landscape. Fanal offers ancient laurel forest; the north coast has cliffs, black sand and natural pools. Funchal, wine cellars and small fishing villages add cultural interest.

Not every experience requires a major hike: many viewpoints are a short walk from the car. Treat demanding trails as dedicated outings, planned around weather and return transport. Madeira is chiefly a destination for scenery and walking; long sandy beaches are scarce.

When to visit

April–June: greenery, flowers and comfortable walking, though mountain weather remains changeable.

July–September: generally warmer and drier, with more visitors. Book entry times for popular trails ahead.

October–March: mild coastal weather but more rain and wind; mountain trails may close. A Funchal forecast does not describe summit conditions.

Travel essentials

Madeira is an autonomous region of Portugal. The currency is the euro; cards are common, but cash helps for small purchases and parking. Portuguese is the local language, with English generally spoken in tourist areas. Power sockets are types C/F.

Drive on the right. Tunnels shorten journeys, but older roads are narrow and steep; a map marker may be far from road access. Buses do not conveniently serve every trailhead. Mountain reception can be patchy, so download maps. Keep valuables out of sight in parked cars.

Many popular Madeira trails charge admission. In this selection these include PR1, PR1.2 (Pico Ruivo), PR11 (Balcões), PR9 (Caldeirão Verde), PR8 (São Lourenço), PR6/6.1 (25 Fontes and Risco) and PR6.2 (Alecrim). All official PR routes require advance timed reservations; fees and concessions depend on the route and visitor. Check conditions and book — SIMplifica. Check trail opening status. These rules do not automatically apply to every island walk. For a through-route, arrange return transport separately. Entry documents depend on citizenship and Schengen rules.

Bring waterproof and warm layers, grippy footwear and sun protection; levadas with tunnels require a head torch. Take repellent if needed: mosquito bites are possible, including a dengue risk. Review routine vaccinations and advice relevant to your health before travelling.

The main outdoor hazards are cliffs, wet rocks, wind and ocean waves. Do not enter natural pools during closures or heavy swell; follow flags and local warnings. Choose a licensed operator for whale and dolphin watching. The emergency number is 112.
