Funchal — old town and market

Culture and history
Regional highlights

Mosaic pavements, painted doors and a lively market introduce everyday island life.

Time needed3–5 hours
Physical effortEasy
Details

Start at Mercado dos Lavradores and its fish hall, then follow Rua de Santa Maria, known for its painted doors, towards São Tiago Fort. In the centre, visit Sé Cathedral for its carved wooden ceiling and stroll along the waterfront. Museums, cafés and the market fit comfortably into a city walk.

Tickets and reservations

No ticket is needed to walk around the city. Museums and some interiors charge separately; check market opening hours before visiting. City parking is paid.

The walk can be shortened. Some streets are steep and paving can be slippery.

Driving access: Almirante Reis car park. Continue on foot through the old town.

Monte Palace — tropical garden

Culture and history · Forests and gardens
Regional highlights

A hillside garden of ponds, azulejo tiles and tropical plants overlooking Funchal.

Time needed2–3 hours
Physical effortModerate
Details

Monte Palace brings together Oriental gardens, bridges, cascades, tree ferns and art collections. Panels of azulejos, Portugal’s traditional painted ceramic tiles, illustrate the country’s history. Expect many steps and changes in level. Reach Monte by bus, taxi or cable car when operating. The traditional wicker toboggan ride has a separate ticket and ends uphill from the coast.

Tickets and reservations

Garden admission is paid. The cable car and wicker toboggan ride have separate tickets; check prices and operating hours before visiting. Parking in Monte is limited.

Sloping paths and steps. Allow a few hours for an unhurried visit.

Driving access: Monte Palace garden entrance. Passenger drop-off point. Bus or taxi is the more convenient option.

Madeira Botanical Garden

Forests and gardens
Worth a stop

Geometric flower beds above the city and plants from several climate zones.

Time needed1.5–2 hours
Physical effortModerate
Details

Choose this garden for its botany: compare native species with plants from dry and tropical regions, follow the colourful formal beds and look down over the harbour. Terraces and stairs; not every path is suitable for visitors with reduced mobility.

Animals and birds
Regional highlights

A chance to see wild dolphins and whales and view the island from the ocean.

Physical effortEasy
Details

Boat trips offer a chance to watch dolphins and whales off Madeira. Sightings depend on the season and the animals’ movements. Choose a vessel and trip length that suit you, and check the departure point and weather cancellation terms before booking.

Driving access: Almirante Reis car park in Funchal. Continue on foot to the marina. Xpoint Madeira meets guests at the entrance to Pontoon A. Other companies use alternative piers; check your ticket before walking to the boat.

Pico do Areeiro — peaks and PR1

Scenery and viewpoints · Outdoor activities
Regional highlights

Views above the clouds and the PR1 mountain trail. The full crossing to Pico Ruivo is one-way and requires booking and return transport.

Time needed1–2 hours; a full day for the traverse
Physical effortChallenging
Details

For a short mountain visit, use the summit viewpoint and the permitted section towards Pedra Rija. The full route follows steps and tunnels around rock walls to Pico Ruivo, usually continuing to Achada do Teixeira. Beyond Pedra Rija, travel is permitted only towards Ruivo; you cannot return the same way. Even the shorter section needs settled weather, grippy footwear and comfort with heights. For the full walk, bring a torch and water and arrange collection in advance.

Tickets and reservations

PR1 requires a timed booking: the return section to Pedra Rija is €4.50, while the full traverse to Ruivo is €10.50 for a standard adult visitor. Parking and transfers are separate. Check restrictions at the upper car park and never bypass a trail closure.

Official PR1 figures to Ruivo are 6.1 km one way and 3 hours; inclusion of stops is unspecified. Continuing to Achada do Teixeira gives about 9.4 km and 643 m ascent, usually 3–5 hours walking according to Earth Trekkers. Allow extra time for stops and transport.

Directions end at the Areeiro parking area. The full PR1 needs collection at Achada do Teixeira rather than a return along the same trail.

Driving access: Pico do Areeiro car park. Upper and lower parking areas have different charges; check signs. Arrange return transport from the far end before starting the full PR1.

Pico Ruivo — from Achada do Teixeira

Scenery and viewpoints
Regional highlights

A shorter ascent to the island’s highest summit without the full PR1 ridge crossing.

Time needed2–3 hours including stops
Physical effortModerate
Details

From Achada do Teixeira, a paved trail climbs to the mountain shelter, followed by steps to the summit. On a clear day, the view spans Madeira’s central mountains. Return along the same path, with no separate transfer needed. The summit can be cold and windy, so check the mountain forecast before setting out.

Tickets and reservations

Book an entry time and pay for PR1.2 through SIMplifica before visiting. Current prices and concessions are shown during booking.

PR1.2 is 5.6 km return. The official time is 1 hour 30 minutes, with stops unspecified; allow 2–3 hours for an unhurried visit. Published ascent is about 351 m and the summit is 1,862 m.

Driving access: Achada do Teixeira car park. Continue on foot along PR1.2.

Balcões — a balcony above the forest

Scenery and viewpoints
Regional highlights

A short forest path follows a levada, an irrigation channel, to the Balcões viewpoint overlooking the mountains.

Time needed1.5–2 hours
Physical effortEasy
Details

This is a rewarding mountain panorama without a long hike. The path follows water beneath the trees from Ribeiro Frio, then opens onto a small platform above the valley. Pico do Areeiro and Pico Ruivo are visible in clear weather. Finches often appear nearby, but should not be fed for photographs. You can also explore Ribeiro Frio forest park; thick mist may hide the distant view.

Tickets and reservations

PR11 requires a timed booking and paid ticket through SIMplifica. Parking at Ribeiro Frio is limited; keep bus stops clear.

PR11: 3 km return, with an official walking time of 1 hour 30 minutes. An easy trail with little ascent.

Driving access: Ribeiro Frio parking on ER103

Caldeirão Verde — tunnels and waterfall

Scenery and viewpoints · Forests and gardens · Outdoor activities
Regional highlights

A narrow levada runs through damp forest and tunnels to a towering waterfall.

Time needed4–6 hours; longer for the extension
Physical effortModerate
Details

The trail starts at the thatched houses in Queimadas forest park. The old water channel passes mossy walls and steep drops, then runs through four tunnels; bring a head torch. Turn back at Caldeirão Verde waterfall for the shorter walk. Continuing to Caldeirão do Inferno makes a much longer outing. Check which sections are open and respect barriers.

Tickets and reservations

Book an entry time and pay for PR9 through SIMplifica. Parking at Queimadas is charged separately; check current prices before visiting.

Caldeirão Verde and back is about 12 km, usually taking 4–6 hours. The longer Inferno route is officially 17.4 km and 6 hours 30 minutes. Parts of the levada path are narrow and exposed.

Driving access: Queimadas paid car park. There is no road to the waterfall.

Rabaçal — 25 Fontes and Risco

Scenery and viewpoints · Forests and gardens · Outdoor activities
Regional highlights

Forest water channels lead to a pool fed by fine streams; a detour adds the tall Risco waterfall.

Time needed4–5 hours including Risco
Physical effortModerate
Details

From Rabaçal house, paths beside irrigation channels lead towards the waterfalls. At 25 Fontes, numerous streams flow down green cliffs into a small pool. A separate branch reaches Risco, which drops from a high rock face. Follow directional signs on narrow sections and do not pass others along the exposed outer edge.

Tickets and reservations

Choose an entry time and the appropriate ticket for PR6 and PR6.1 through SIMplifica. The shuttle to Rabaçal house is charged separately. Check signs for parking arrangements beside ER105.

Official PR6 figures are 8.6 km return and 3 hours. From the upper car park with Risco, the walk is about 11 km with 385 m ascent according to Earth Trekkers; allow 4–5 hours for sightseeing. The shuttle shortens the walking distance.

Driving access: Upper Rabaçal car park, ER105. Continue on foot or by paid shuttle, not by private car.

Canyoning — Ribeiro Frio waterfalls

Outdoor activities
Complex logistics

Canyoning combines abseiling beside waterfalls with a guided descent through a mountain gorge. The route depends on the group’s experience and water levels.

Time needed3–5 hours including preparation
Physical effortChallenging
Details

Madeira offers outings from introductory descents to technical canyons. Choose by experience, age limits and water conditions. Ribeiro Frio is one activity area, but operators may change canyons because of rain or safety. This is not an independent waterfall walk. Ask whether jumps are compulsory, what equipment is included and how cold the water will be.

Tickets and reservations

Book with a licensed operator. Check whether a wetsuit, helmet, insurance and transfers are included, and confirm the meeting point and parking when booking.

Wet terrain, rope descents and cold water. Duration and difficulty depend on the programme.

Driving access: Parking area by the tables on ER103, entrance 3 to upper Ribeiro Frio canyon. This is one approach to the canyon; book a guided descent. Alternative entrances and transfers from Funchal are available. Agree this meeting point with your guide, as your programme may use a different canyon section.

Ponta de São Lourenço — volcanic peninsula

Scenery and viewpoints · Outdoor activities
Regional highlights

A dry volcanic peninsula with ocean on both sides of the trail, colourful cliffs and open views.

Time needed3–4 hours including stops
Physical effortModerate
Details

This eastern peninsula contrasts sharply with the island’s damp forests. Ocean views flank the trail, and the cliffs expose layers and dark volcanic veins. Walk to the first viewpoints and return, or continue to Casa do Sardinha; take extra branches only where signs permit. There is almost no shade, and strong wind can make exposed sections unpleasant. Tickets and reservations

Book an entry time and pay for PR8 through SIMplifica. Check open sections and diversions before setting out. At Baía d’Abra, keep the bus turning circle clear when parking.

The full route is about 8 km return. Allow 3–4 hours with stops; the distance depends on which branches are open.

Driving access: Baía d’Abra car park, PR8 trailhead. Continue on foot.

Fanal — ancient laurel forest

Scenery and viewpoints · Forests and gardens

Twisted old trees among meadows, especially atmospheric in mist.

Details

Fanal is part of Madeira’s ancient laurel forest. Old trees with twisting branches stand in meadows that give way to dense woodland. A short walk near the forest station offers a good introduction; mist makes the silhouettes particularly striking. Plan the longer PR13 and PR14 routes separately, and keep your distance from grazing cattle.

Tickets and reservations

Check forest access and parking arrangements before visiting. PR13 and PR14 require advance booking and tickets through SIMplifica.

A short forest walk without completing a full PR route. It is easy to lose your bearings in mist.

Driving access: Fanal forest station car park, ER209

Why visit

Madeira draws visitors for peaks above the clouds, laurel forests and steep Atlantic cliffs. One of the best ways to explore is along a levada: these irrigation channels have paths beside them leading through forests and towards waterfalls.

Choose Pico do Areeiro and Pico Ruivo for mountain panoramas, or São Lourenço for dry volcanic slopes and ocean views on both sides of the trail. Fanal has old laurel trees, while the north coast offers black-sand beaches and natural lava pools. In Funchal and the fishing villages, explore old streets, try the local food and taste Madeira, the island’s fortified wine.

Many viewpoints are a short walk from a car park. Allow a separate day for a long mountain route and arrange your return transport in advance. Madeira is particularly rewarding for walking and scenic drives; long sandy beaches are scarce.

When to visit

April–June: greenery, flowers and generally comfortable walking temperatures. Mountain weather remains changeable.

July–September: usually warmer and drier, with more visitors. Book popular trails and accommodation ahead.

October–March: coastal temperatures stay mild, but rain and strong winds are more frequent; mountain trails may close. Before a hike, check a mountain forecast rather than relying on conditions in Funchal.

Travel essentials

Madeira is an autonomous region of Portugal. The currency is the euro; cards are widely accepted, but cash is useful for small purchases and parking. Portuguese is the main language, and English is commonly spoken in tourist areas. Socket types are C/F.

Drive on the right. Tunnels shorten journeys, but older roads are often narrow and steep. Buses may not conveniently serve your chosen trailhead, so check access and return transport ahead. Mobile reception can drop out in the mountains; download maps and do not leave valuables in your car.

Many popular trails charge admission. In this selection, these include PR1, PR1.2 (Pico Ruivo), PR11 (Balcões), PR9 (Caldeirão Verde), PR8 (São Lourenço), PR6/6.1 (25 Fontes and Risco) and PR6.2 (Alecrim). Official routes marked PR require advance timed reservations through SIMplifica; fees and concessions depend on the route and visitor. These rules do not apply to every walk on the island. Check that your trail is open before setting out; booking and trail-status links are below. Entry documents depend on nationality and Schengen rules.

Bring a waterproof jacket, warm clothing, footwear with good grip and sun protection; levada walks with tunnels also call for a head torch. Take repellent if needed: dengue can be transmitted by mosquitoes on the island. Review routine vaccinations and individual health advice before travelling.

The main outdoor hazards are cliffs, wet rocks, strong winds and ocean waves. Do not enter natural pools when access is closed or the swell is heavy; follow local warnings. Choose a licensed operator for whale and dolphin watching. The emergency number is 112.
