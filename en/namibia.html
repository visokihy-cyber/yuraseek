Why visit

Desert and light. Deadvlei, dunes and Spitzkoppe change with the light. Short walks offer memorable views; NamibRand walking programmes and Naukluft trails provide deeper exploration.

Unhurried wildlife. At Etosha, waiting by water matters as much as driving. Northeastern river parks complement the safari, while the coast reveals seals, flamingos and tiny dune creatures.

History and people. Twyfelfontein and Brandberg preserve rock art. The community-run Damara museum explains traditional skills; Windhoek, Lüderitz and Kolmanskop reveal complex national history. Agree cultural visits with participants rather than treating people as scenery.

When to visit

May–October: generally drier, with wildlife gathering at waterholes. Desert nights can be cold, while late dry-season days are hot.

November–April: greener scenery and birds after rain, but heat, local flooding and changing roads demand flexibility. Water levels particularly affect the northeast.

The coast is cooler than inland areas and can be foggy year-round. The multi-day Fish River trail has its own season and may close independently of viewpoints; check NWR.

Travel essentials

The currency is the Namibian dollar (NAD); South African rand is also widely accepted. English is official and widely used in tourism, alongside Afrikaans, German and local languages. Drive on the left. Sockets are types D/M. Settlements are far apart and communications and medical care can be limited: carry water, fuel reserves and offline maps. Keep belongings out of sight in vehicles and avoid night driving.

Large animals require distance in safari parks; crocodiles and hippos inhabit the Kunene and northeastern rivers. Cold water, surf and currents are everyday coastal hazards; swim only where permitted and safe. Discuss vaccination and malaria prevention for northern areas before travelling; bring repellent when needed. Sun protection and a warm layer belong in the same luggage.
