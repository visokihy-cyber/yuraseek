Namib Desert

Sossusvlei and Deadvlei

Scenery and viewpoints
Regional highlights

White clay, dark trees and orange dunes create Namibia’s classic desert scene.

Time neededHalf a day to a day
Physical effortModerate
Details

Walk from the drop-off area to Deadvlei’s clay pan. Climbing Big Daddy dune is a separate, more demanding option. Morning light brings out the dune ridges, but temperatures rise quickly after sunrise. Sossusvlei and Deadvlei are neighbouring pans. Do not touch or climb the old trees. The driving route ends at the two-wheel-drive car park; arrange a shuttle for the final sandy section.

Tickets and reservations

Paid Namib-Naukluft entry is required for visitors and vehicles. The shuttle over the final sandy stretch costs extra; confirm current access rules at Sesriem. Book accommodation separately.

Driving access: the 2x4 car park through Sesriem gate. Arrange the onward shuttle; Deadvlei also requires a walk.

Naukluft — mountain trails

Scenery and viewpoints · Outdoor activities
Complex logistics

Rocky gorges, mountain zebras and occasional shade make a substantial hiking day.

Time neededFull day
Physical effortStrenuous
Details

In the Naukluft Mountains, choose the Olive Trail or the longer Waterkloof Trail. Rocky sections require sure footing, and chained passages need particular care. Start early and carry water. For a gentler walk, ask about short open routes near camp. Multi-day hikes require separate preparation and bookings.

Tickets and reservations

Register and pay at Naukluft Camp. Multi-day routes need separate reservations; check availability of day trails.

NWR lists Olive Trail as 10 km and about 4 hours, and Waterkloof as 17 km and 6–7 hours. Extra stops and conditions can extend these estimates; Olive includes demanding rocky sections.

Drive to Naukluft Camp to register; leave the vehicle at the agreed trailhead

Moon Landscape and welwitschias

Scenery and viewpoints
Worth a stop

Eroded rocky valleys give way to plains of unusual long-lived plants.

Time neededHalf a day
Physical effortEasy
Details

The drive crosses eroded rocky valleys and plains dotted with welwitschias. Each plant has just two leaves, which grow throughout its life and gradually split into long ribbons. Obtain a permit and map, then follow the marked stops. Stay on authorised routes to protect fragile lichens and surface roots. Check river crossings after rain.

Tickets and reservations

Obtain a MEFT permit in Swakopmund before setting out and check which roads are open. Permits are not issued along the route.

Route first to the MEFT office in Swakopmund for the permit and access map. Follow that map for the actual drive.

Brandberg — White Lady rock art

Culture and history
Worth a stop

A rocky valley walk leads to a celebrated group of paintings.

Time needed2–3 hours
Physical effortModerate
Details

The historical name White Lady can be misleading: the central figure is often interpreted as a man, possibly taking part in a ritual. Your guide explains the paintings and their possible meanings. Bring water and start early, as heat makes the walk more demanding. The trail lies at the foot of Brandberg and does not climb its highest summit. Follow the site’s protection rules when viewing the panel.

Tickets and reservations

Admission and the required guide are charged. Other gorges and summit climbs need separate permission and preparation.

Driving access: the White Lady trail entrance at the end of D2359, not the similarly named lodge. Continue with a guide.

Etosha — waterholes and safari

Animals and birds
Regional highlights

Wildlife viewing centres on waterholes and an immense pale salt pan.

Time needed2–3 days
Physical effortEasy
Details

Choose a few waterholes and allow time to watch wildlife. In the dry season, animals gather at the water; after rain, vegetation greens and wildlife spreads across the park. Okaukuejo, Halali and Namutoni camps are bases for exploring different areas. An overnight stay allows an evening at a camp’s floodlit waterhole. Independent night driving is prohibited; leave your vehicle only in designated places.

Tickets and reservations

Visitor and vehicle entry fees apply. Book campsites or rooms ahead; guided drives cost extra. Respect park and camp gate hours.

Driving access: Andersson Gate, a convenient southern entrance. If staying by another gate, adjust to your booking rather than routing through a closed park.

Details

Options on the Orange River range from short canoe trips to multi-day journeys with riverside camps. You do the paddling, and the effort depends on the current, wind and heat. A guide helps with rapids and camping arrangements. As the river forms an international border, confirm your departure bank, required documents and return transfer in advance.

Tickets and reservations

Advance booking is required. Confirm included equipment, age requirements, transfers and documents for the chosen departure bank.

Driving access: Bundi Camp on the Namibian bank if booked with this operator. Other trips use their own base and directions.

Why visit

Desert landscapes. Visit Namibia for towering dunes, Deadvlei’s white clay pan and dead trees, and the granite formations of Spitzkoppe. Morning and evening light bring out the contours of sand and rock. Walking routes in NamibRand Nature Reserve and the Naukluft Mountains offer a closer look at the desert.

Wildlife. In Etosha National Park, watch animals on game drives and at waterholes. Northeastern parks introduce river landscapes; along the coast, look for seals and flamingos, or join a dune excursion to find small animals adapted to life in the sand.

History and culture. Twyfelfontein and the Brandberg massif preserve rock art. At the Damara Living Museum, local people demonstrate traditional crafts and share their culture. The architecture of Windhoek and Lüderitz, together with the abandoned mining settlement of Kolmanskop, offers an introduction to the country’s colonial past.

When to visit

May–October: generally dry, with animals easier to spot at waterholes. Desert nights can be cold, while daytime temperatures rise towards the end of the dry season.

November–April: rain brings green vegetation and abundant birdlife. Heat and flooding can complicate travel, making some roads impassable. In the northeast, check water levels and road access before setting out.

The coast is cooler than inland areas, with fog possible throughout the year. The multi-day hike through Fish River Canyon operates within a set season, with restrictions that differ from those for the viewpoints. Check dates and conditions with Namibia Wildlife Resorts.

Travel essentials

The currency is the Namibian dollar (NAD); South African rand is also widely accepted. English is the official language and is generally sufficient in tourist areas. Afrikaans, German and local languages are also spoken. Socket types are D/M.

Drive on the left. Distances between settlements are large, and remote areas have limited mobile reception and medical care. Plan fuel stops, carry water and download maps. Avoid night driving and do not leave valuables in your vehicle.

In safari parks, keep your distance from large animals and follow rules on leaving your vehicle. Crocodiles and hippos occur in the Kunene and northeastern rivers. Cold water, powerful surf and currents are coastal hazards; seek local advice before swimming.

Check vaccination advice before departure and discuss malaria prevention with a clinician if travelling to the north. Bring repellent if needed. Pack sun protection for hot days and warm clothing for cold mornings and evenings. Visa requirements depend on nationality. Health and safety · Entry requirements.
