Why visit

New Zealand draws visitors for open space and varied landscapes: volcanic plateaux, turquoise lakes, snow-capped peaks, forests and fiords. Scenic drives are part of the appeal, whether beside Lake Pukaki, towards the village of Glenorchy or through the mountains to Milford Sound.

On the South Island, see Aoraki / Mount Cook, the country’s highest mountain, walk to alpine lakes and glacial valleys, or cruise through a fiord beneath cliffs and waterfalls. The North Island offers Rotorua’s geysers and thermal pools, Tongariro’s volcanoes, Māori cultural experiences and the bays of Northland and the Coromandel Peninsula.

Build a holiday around hiking or take a road trip with viewpoints and shorter walks along the way. For mountain and lake photography, allow several days in one area: cloud and wind can quickly change the visibility and light.

When to visit

December–February: summer brings opportunities for coastal trips and mountain walks. Book popular accommodation and multi-day walks ahead.

March–May: autumn colours, especially on the South Island. Days become shorter and cooler towards the end of the season.

June–August: winter brings snow to the mountains; walking conditions depend on elevation and weather.

September–November: spring weather is changeable. Valleys turn green, but high trails may still be covered in snow.

Travel essentials

The currency is the New Zealand dollar (NZD), and cards are widely accepted. English is the main language of communication. Tourist infrastructure is well developed, but remote areas have fewer petrol stations and patchy mobile reception: download maps and plan fuel stops. Sockets are type I.

Drive on the left. Allow extra time on narrow, winding roads, especially in the mountains. If your driving licence is not in English, check the translation requirements. Do not leave valuables in a parked car. Outdoors, account for rapid weather changes, strong sun and current trail conditions.

Before departure, check whether you need a visa or NZeTA, an electronic travel authority; requirements depend on nationality and the circumstances of your visit. Complete the New Zealand Traveller Declaration (NZTD). Declare food and used outdoor equipment, and clean soil from boots and gear beforehand. Choose insurance covering your planned activities.

Review routine vaccinations before travelling. Bring a waterproof jacket, warm clothing even in summer and sun protection; take sandfly repellent if needed.

Keep at least 20 metres from seals and sea lions. Great white sharks occur around the coast, including near seal colonies; attacks are rare. Check local warnings before swimming.
