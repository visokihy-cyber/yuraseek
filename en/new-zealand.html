Why visit

Come for a sense of space and scenery that changes throughout a single journey: volcanic plateaux, turquoise lakes, snowy peaks, damp forests, fiords and ocean bays. The experience starts on the road, beside Lake Pukaki, on the way to Glenorchy or among the mountains en route to Milford Sound.

The South Island is above all about mountains and water: see Aoraki / Mount Cook, walk to alpine lakes, explore glacial valleys and cruise beneath sheer cliffs and waterfalls. The North Island adds Rotorua’s geysers and colourful thermal pools, Tongariro’s volcanic landscapes, Māori culture and the coasts of Northland and the Coromandel.

Build a trip around hiking, photography or a relaxed road journey. Many views are reached from the road or short paths; longer trails take you deeper into the mountains. Weather changes the scene: cloud hides summits and wind breaks reflections, while rain brings waterfalls to life. The main reward is experiencing this variety of landscapes from within.

When to visit

December–February: summer, coastal visits and mountain walks.

March–May: autumn colours, becoming cooler towards the end of the season.

June–August: winter and mountain snow. September–November: changeable spring weather; high trails may still hold snow.

Travel essentials

The currency is the New Zealand dollar (NZD), and cards are widely accepted. English is the main language of communication. Tourist infrastructure is well developed, but reception and petrol stations are less frequent in remote areas: download maps and keep enough fuel.

Drive on the left. Narrow, winding roads take time, especially in the mountains; a licence without English text requires an accepted translation. The country is generally calm, but take valuables out of parked cars. Outdoors, plan for rapid weather changes, strong sun and current trail conditions.

Before departure, check visa or NZeTA requirements for your citizenship and circumstances, and complete the New Zealand Traveller Declaration. Declare food and used outdoor equipment; clean soil from boots and gear beforehand. Choose insurance covering your planned activities.

No vaccination certificates are required for entry; review routine vaccinations before travelling. Bring a waterproof jacket, a warm layer even in summer and sun protection; take sandfly repellent if needed. Power sockets are type I.

Keep at least 20 metres from seals and sea lions, which can bite. Great white sharks occur around the coast, particularly near seal colonies; attacks are rare. Observe local advice before swimming.
