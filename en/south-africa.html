Table Mountain

Scenery and viewpoints
Regional highlights

The plateau brings Cape Town, the ocean and mountain chain into one panorama.

Time neededHalf day
Physical effortEasy with the cableway
Details

After the cable-car ride, follow short paths between viewpoints rather than staying beside the station. Cloud can obscure landmarks, making it easy to lose your bearings, so longer trails need preparation. Hiking up is a separate mountain outing, much harder than strolling at the summit. For a first visit, choose a clear weather window and allow time for queues and the descent.

Tickets and reservations

The cableway requires a ticket; advance purchase is recommended. Wind and maintenance affect operation. The suggested park-and-ride has a free shuttle; check its timetable.

Drive to Lower Tafelberg Road parking, then take the shuttle to the lower station and cableway

Robben Island

Culture and history
Regional highlights

The former prison makes the history of apartheid tangible through a place and individual lives.

Time needed4 hours
Physical effortEasy
Details

The visit includes a ferry crossing, a tour of the island and a visit to the former prison. Guides explain Nelson Mandela’s imprisonment and other chapters in Robben Island’s history. Visits follow a set tour programme. Allow extra time for check-in and boarding; rough seas can change the schedule.

Tickets and reservations

Book a specific ferry departure in advance and arrive at least 30 minutes early. Waterfront parking is separate; check required identification and cancellation rules with the museum.

Drive to Clock Tower Parking at the Waterfront, walk to Nelson Mandela Gateway and board the ferry

Kirstenbosch

Forests and gardens
Worth a stop

Time needed2–4 hours

Details

Hermanus — whales from shore

Animals and birds · Scenery and viewpoints
Worth a stop

A coastal path lets you look for whales from land while exploring the bay.

Time neededHalf day
Physical effortEasy
Details

Southern right whales approach the coast during their breeding season. Watch for blows, backs and movement at the surface; binoculars and patience help. Walk part of the Cliff Path or book a licensed boat trip. Outside the season, cliffs and ocean remain worthwhile, but whales cannot be promised.

Robberg

Scenery and viewpoints · Animals and birds · Outdoor activities
Regional highlights

A rocky peninsula combines ocean views, a dune and a seal colony.

Time needed2–5 hours
Physical effortEasy to strenuous
Details

The short Gap Circuit explores the coastal rock formations, Witsand reaches a sandy isthmus, and Point Circuit continues to the peninsula’s far tip. The full loop is considerably more demanding than the short walk. Seals are usually viewed from the cliffs above. Check tides, wind and open trail sections before setting out.

Tickets and reservations

A paid CapeNature day permit is required and can be arranged through its booking system. Fountain Shack accommodation requires a separate reservation.

CapeNature lists Gap Circuit as 2.1 km/about 30 minutes, Witsand as 5.5 km/about 2 hours, and Point Circuit as 9.2 km/about 4 hours. These are standard estimates; stops and conditions can add time.

Drive to the parking area inside Robberg’s entrance; the three main loops start here

Why visit

Ocean and mountains. Visit South Africa for rocky headlands, coastal scenery and mountain hiking. Take in the coast from Cape Point’s viewpoints, follow trails above the ocean on the Robberg Peninsula, or explore the high cliffs and valleys of the Drakensberg.

Wildlife. Choose Kruger for the variety of animals on safari, Addo especially for elephants, or Kgalagadi for wildlife in a semi-desert landscape. On the coast, see African penguins and seasonal whales near Hermanus, or dive among coral reefs and marine life at Sodwana Bay.

Culture and history. San rock paintings and the remains of the ancient kingdom of Mapungubwe reveal southern Africa’s past. Apartheid museums explain the history and consequences of racial segregation. In the wine valleys, visit estates, taste local wines and food, and explore historic farmhouses.

When to visit

Cape Town and the Western Cape: November–March is generally warmer and drier; June–August is cooler and wetter. Strong winds can suspend cable-car services and boat trips.

Northeastern safaris: May–September is usually drier, with thinner vegetation making animals easier to spot. The southern summer, from December to February, brings more greenery and rain. The Drakensberg can have summer thunderstorms and winter frost or snow.

Whales and wildflowers: whales are most often seen near Hermanus from June to November. Namaqua’s flowering season usually falls in August and September; its timing and abundance depend on winter rainfall. Before a wildflower trip, check where flowers are blooming that season.

Travel essentials

The currency is the South African rand (ZAR). Many languages are spoken; English is widely used in tourist areas. Socket types are M/N, with D also found. Cities and major tourist routes have good infrastructure; remote areas have fewer fuel stops and medical facilities, and mobile reception can be unreliable.

Drive on the left. Robbery and carjacking are significant concerns: ask your accommodation about a safe route there before arrival, do not leave belongings in the car, and avoid night driving or walking alone in unfamiliar areas.

On safari, leave your vehicle only where permitted and never feed baboons. Hippos and crocodiles occur in northeastern rivers. Sharks and strong currents are possible coastal hazards: choose lifeguarded beaches and follow warnings and swimming restrictions.

Check vaccination advice before travelling and discuss malaria prevention with a clinician if your route includes risk areas in Limpopo, Mpumalanga or northeastern KwaZulu-Natal.
